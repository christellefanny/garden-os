# Welcoming interior pages

Plant Vault, Growing Calendar, Tasks, and Garden Journal share an optional illustrated InteriorHeader variant. Other page headers retain their existing rendering. Scoped styles add warm ivory headers, restrained seasonal accents, small statistics cards, quiet toolbars, and softly framed content. Mobile headers show compact art above the controls. All existing collection, calendar, reminder, task, photo, observation, and long-form journal actions remain available. No database or stored-data changes.

## Artwork

Four original watercolor/gouache vignettes were created with the built-in image generation tool and optimized as 900 × 600 WebP assets. Decorative illustrations describe each page, not the user’s actual plants or records. The headers fade artwork edges into the cream surface.

### public/interiors/vault.webp

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset: premium botanical garden application header vignette, landscape 3:2 composition. Three terracotta seedling pots with realistic tender tomato and lettuce leaves, a few unlettered cream seed envelopes, a small scatter of seeds. Restrained botanical still life. Sophisticated hand-painted watercolor and fine gouache on warm ivory paper #faf6ed. Realistic plant forms, finely observed textures, expressive natural pigment, soft morning light, muted leafy greens with gentle blush and terracotta. Entire still life centered, occupying middle 65 percent of canvas, plenty of blank ivory paper all around, edges softly dissolve into paper. Elegant, airy, uncluttered, a professional botanical lifestyle publication. No lettering, no words, no UI, no border, no cartoon, no flat vector, no photographs.

## Validation

65 unit tests, TypeScript, lint (one existing native-image warning), and production build. Browser QA uses isolated local fixture records only; all four pages checked at 1536, 1024, 768, 390, and 320 px; four-season accessibility checks, distinct decoded assets, collection tools and add-plant form, calendar dialogs and month navigation, task completion persistence, observation save, and journal/photo dialogs. Desktop and mobile screenshots were visually reviewed.
